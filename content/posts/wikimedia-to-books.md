I upload a good amount of photos to the Wikimedia Commons. For those unaware,
there is an organization behind the famous online encyclopedia called the 
Wikimedia Foundation that manages several projects that promote free information. 
Some of these project include:
* Wikivoyage - a free Wikipedia style travel guide
* Wikidata - a knowledge base of structured data
* Wikimedia Commons - a free-to-use image repository
* Wikibooks - free English language textbooks
* And more...

I am personally most interested in Wikimedia Commons which hosts all of the 
images on Wikipedia. The Wikimedia Commons is great because all photos shared
there must be distributed under some creative commons license or in the public
domain, meaning that they can be used by anyone for free 
(with some modest restrictions).

I found out about the Commons during my undergrad. I took a history of 
technology course, and the instructor required us to use the Commons for a 
research project on the worlds fairs. While browsing, I discovered you could
just upload your own photos as long as you could justify that they had some
educational use.

During COVID-19 I had amassed a decent collection of bird and nature photos
from a local water reservoir. I also had some photos of government buildings in
Washington D.C. from a family vacation, and I was getting into macro
photography, so I had a good amount of insect photos as well. I decided that
they might be useful some people.

## Licensing 

When it came to licensing images, there are several types of creative commons
licenses to choose from. Creative commons licenses fall under the broader topic
of "copyleft" licenses. Unlike standard copyright licenses, copyleft allow the
creator to retain some control over how their intellectual property is used while
also offering greater freedom to users. 

In my case, I opted for the CC BY-SA 4.0 license for my work. This license 
allows users to:
* **Share:** users can share the images with anyone they like in any media, for
 any purpose, even commercially.
* **Remix:** users can modify, edit, remix, or otherwise alter the original work 
 to create derivative works.

But has two main restrictions:
* **Attribution:** users must attribute the original creator (me) and indicate
 any changes made to the work.
* **Share Alike:** any derivative or remixed work must also be distributed under
 the CC BY-SA 4.0 license. 

Now, I have gone back and fourth on licensing my photos in this way. Part
of me wants to just publish my photos in the public domain (or under the 
CC 0 license) which puts no restrictions at all on the work. However, I think 
the Share Alike clause is quite important.

As an information professional, I dislike many aspects modern copyright law. I
find it far too restrictive, and I believe that anything that limits access 
to information to be counter-productive.

If people are able to make money off of my work, I think that's great! Isn't it?
